## Beyond GNSS: a survey and tutorial on satellite-based radio frequency (RF) geolocation and emitter fingerprinting

- **Article title:** Beyond GNSS: a survey and tutorial on satellite-based radio frequency (RF) geolocation and emitter fingerprinting
- **Authors:** Nermine Hendy, Bisma Manzoor, Ferdi Ganda Kurnia, Fernando Moya Caceres, Akram Al-Hourani
- **Journal:** npj Wireless Technology, Volume 2, Article number 37
- **Publication year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s44459-026-00045-y

---

## Article Objective

This article answers two fundamental questions in satellite-based RF sensing:

1. **"Where is the transmitter?"** — Geolocation of a ground-based transmitter (unknown station) using physical measurements of the uplink signal received by satellites.
2. **"Who is the transmitter?"** — Identification and authentication of the device through unique physical-layer characteristics (RF Fingerprinting).

**Motivation:** Despite its ubiquity, GNSS is vulnerable to jamming, spoofing, and signal blockage. In GNSS-denied environments, satellite-based non-cooperative geolocation can serve as an alternative or complement, since it requires neither cooperation from the target device nor the presence of GNSS on it.

---

## Brief Summary

The article presents an integrated three-stage framework:

1. **Spectrum Detection:** Traditional methods (Energy Detection, CFAR, Matched Filter, Cyclostationary) and machine learning methods (CNN, LSTM, clustering).
2. **Geolocation:** Four main measurements — RSS (power), TDoA (time), Doppler (frequency), AoA (angle) — and their hybrid combinations; in either Batch (offline) or Sequential (online tracking) form.
3. **Fingerprinting:** Extrinsic (environmental) and Intrinsic (hardware-related, such as IQ Imbalance and CFO) features for authentication and anti-spoofing.

# 2
## A Simple and Efficient Estimator for Hyperbolic Location

- **Article title:** A Simple and Efficient Estimator for Hyperbolic Location
- **Authors:** Y. T. Chan, K. C. Ho
- **Journal:** IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, Volume 42, Issue 8, Pages 1905–1915
- **Publication year:** 1994
- **DOI:** 10.1109/78.301830

---

## Article Objective

To propose a **non-iterative**, **closed-form** estimator for TDOA-based source localization that approximates the Maximum Likelihood (ML) estimator, attains the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) in the small error region, and is computationally more efficient than iterative methods such as Taylor-series.

---

## Brief Summary

The core idea of the paper is known as the **Two-Stage Weighted Least Squares (TWLS)** method. The algorithm proceeds as follows:

1. **Linearization:** By introducing an auxiliary variable (the range from the source to the reference sensor, denoted as r₁), the nonlinear TDOA equations are transformed into a linear system. The unknown vector contains the source coordinates (x, y) and the auxiliary variable r₁.

2. **Stage 1 (Weighted Least Squares):** Assuming the source coordinates and the auxiliary variable are independent, a rough estimate of the unknown vector is obtained via weighted least squares.

3. **Stage 2 (Constraint Application):** The known relationship between the source coordinates and the auxiliary variable (i.e., the range from the source to the reference sensor equals the Euclidean distance) is imposed as a constraint. A second weighted least squares step refines the estimate.

4. **Final Estimate:** The source position is computed by adding the reference sensor coordinates to the square root of the refined estimate.

---

## Key Results

- **Improvement over Spherical Interpolation (SI):** The proposed method achieves better accuracy than SI.
- **Higher Noise Threshold:** Compared to the Divide and Conquer (DAC) method, the proposed method maintains CRLB accuracy over a wider range of noise levels.
- **Lower Complexity:** Much lighter than the Taylor-series method because it does not require iteration.
- **Closed-Form Covariance:** The paper provides an analytical expression for the covariance matrix of the estimation error.

---

## Relation to Other Papers

- This paper is the **foundation of the TWLS method**, which is referenced in later works such as the CWLS with bias reduction and the DNSP method.
